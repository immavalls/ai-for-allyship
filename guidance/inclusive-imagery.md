# Guidance: inclusive imagery for technical communities

Use this with image-generating assistants, and separately when reviewing generated artwork. It is **guidance**, not a guarantee or demographic audit.

## Before generation

- Ask for the scene, venue, activity, intended audience, medium, and desired realism.
- Describe a naturally varied mix of people across visible skin tones, ages, and gender presentation, **without tokenizing or exaggerating identity markers**.
- Distribute meaningful roles (speaking, coding, operating, leading, learning, mentoring) without linking jobs or expertise to gender, age, ethnicity, or appearance.
- Show realistic interactions, clothing, expressions, mobility and access needs where contextually appropriate; don't turn disability into an aesthetic prop.
- Do not infer or claim anyone's precise identity, gender, race, or disability from an image.
- Never imply one generated image verifies the actual demographic composition of an event.

## Example prompt

> Documentary-style illustration of an informal cloud-native community meetup in Barcelona. A naturally varied group of adults of different ages, skin tones, and gender presentations exchange ideas around a technical demo; distribute speaking, technical, and organizing roles without stereotypes. People have realistic, individually distinctive appearances and expressions. Include a welcoming social atmosphere, food and drinks, accessible circulation space, and no branded logos or fabricated event claims. Avoid a homogeneous group of young white men and avoid diversity as a staged lineup.

## After generation: review and iterate

1. Is any visible group unintentionally homogeneous given the intended setting?
2. Are leadership and technical roles stereotyped or distributed narrowly?
3. Do people look individually distinct, or like repeated stock characters?
4. Is anyone reduced to a decorative diversity cue?
5. Would a person viewing the image reasonably infer the event welcomes a variety of participants? This is a qualitative judgment, not a score.
6. If an issue is found, describe **what to change**, regenerate, and review again.

**Caution:** Avoid visually classifying people into precise demographic categories; review patterns and role assignments, not identities or percentages guessed from appearance. For real photography, represent the actual event accurately and obtain relevant consent/permissions.
