# Guidance: inclusive community writing

Use this document as context when generating **or reviewing** community emails, CFPs, meetup descriptions, announcements, and social posts.

## Goals

- Invite participation without using status, seniority, academic credentials, employment, or insider knowledge as proxies for belonging.
- Choose a friendly, respectful, non-aggressive, non-elitist tone while maintaining accuracy about genuinely advanced or limited-capacity events.
- Say who an event serves, what participants can expect, and what support is available. Do not promise inclusion or accessibility measures unless organizers confirm them.
- Explain specialist terms where helpful; welcome questions and newcomers without diminishing experienced attendees.
- Avoid assuming participants' gender, age, ethnicity, ability, citizenship, family situation, or career status.
- Respect the writer's voice and the language's grammar; do not force awkward wording.

## Watch for patterns

- **Gatekeeping without justification:** 'exclusive community of top-tier engineers', 'only the best', 'high-signal, low-noise'. These can alienate people who would otherwise contribute. If eligibility truly is restricted, explain criteria transparently rather than hiding them.
- **Aggressive competition:** 'crush the competition', 'real engineers only', 'don't waste our time'. Suggest collaborative alternatives.
- **Unnecessary assumptions:** 'he' for a generic expert, 'guys' for everyone, or gendered professional group labels when not needed.
- **Unclear access:** inaccessible registration, no pricing information, unexplained acronyms, presumed prerequisites.

## Suggested process

1. Ask for audience, purpose, channel, location, relevant access information and language. Work with the facts given; ask concise questions for blockers.
2. Draft a readable, accurate message.
3. Check for unnecessary exclusion signals, assumptions, and missing event information.
4. Offer a revised draft plus **brief, specific explanations** of meaningful changes; don't turn every text into a lecture.
5. Where the source lacks details (Code of Conduct, access, support), flag them for verification — **never fabricate policies or links**.

## Example

**Original:** 'Join our exclusive gathering of top-tier engineers to discuss cutting-edge AI.'

**Alternative:** 'Join fellow engineers, researchers, and curious community members to explore recent AI developments together. Experience with the topic is welcome, but you don't need to be an expert to take part.'

*Adjust the alternative if the event truly has justified prerequisites.*
